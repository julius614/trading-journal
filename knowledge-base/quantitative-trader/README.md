## 1. What a quant trader is

- A **quantitative trader** runs algorithmic strategies in **live markets** and makes
  **real-time trading decisions**. They use large datasets and mathematical models to find
  patterns, inefficiencies and opportunities for systematic trading. (article)
- They **design, test and run rule-based strategies**, then execute and monitor them live. (article)
- The trader **owns the strategy end-to-end** and **manages risk in real time**. (article)
- Quant traders often **combine systematic models with discretionary judgement** to spot
  and act on market inefficiencies. (related)

## 2. Responsibilities & a typical day

Typical day (article):
1. Analyse fresh market data.
2. Fine-tune the trading algorithms.
3. Backtest strategies.
4. Monitor key risk metrics.

Core responsibilities (article):

| Area | What it involves |
|---|---|
| Strategy development | Turning ideas into rule-based, testable strategies |
| Signal research | Finding and validating predictive signals in the data |
| Risk management | Live limits, exposure and drawdown control |
| Execution optimisation | Reducing slippage and costs when trades go live |
| Model monitoring | Catching decay or breakage in live models |
| Collaboration | Working with researchers, developers and risk teams |

## 3. Quant role map

| Role | Focus | One-liner |
|---|---|---|
| **Quant researcher / analyst** | Research | Digs through historical data for signals and builds predictive models; spots inefficiencies |
| **Quant trader** | Live trading | Takes the models live, manages risk in real time, makes fast calls when markets turn |
| **Quant developer** | Engineering | Builds the systems that develop, test, deploy and run the models |
| **Quant risk manager** | Risk | Measures and limits the risk of the firm's strategies |
| **Portfolio manager** | Allocation | Owns the capital and performance across strategies |

(article + related)

## 4. Where quant traders work

Proprietary trading firms, hedge funds, asset managers, and the trading desks of
investment banks. (related)

## 5. Skills

**Technical**
- Programming: **Python** (the most common), **R**, **C++** (for low-latency work). (article)
- Maths: **probability, statistics, linear algebra, calculus, statistical inference**. (article/related)
- **Mathematical modelling and data analytics.** (article)

**Market**
- A solid grasp of **financial markets** and **market sense**. (article)
- Experience with complex products: **options, ETFs, futures**. (article)
- **Risk management.** (article)

**Behavioural**
- **Making good decisions under time pressure** and in high-stress conditions. (article)
- Fast, probability-based thinking. (article)

## 6. Education & career path

- **There is no single fixed path.** (article)
- Typical degrees: **mathematics, statistics, computer science, engineering, physics,
  economics**. Some firms prefer a **master's degree**. (article)
- Common route (article):
  1. Build strong skills in **programming (usually Python), statistics and markets**.
  2. Get hands-on experience through **projects or internships**.
  3. Many add a **structured programme or certification** in algorithmic/quant trading
     (QuantInsti promotes its EPAT here).
- Progression (related): many start in **research roles** → gain **more autonomy** →
  **manage larger portfolios** → occasionally **start their own funds**.
- Background fit (related): people from an IT background often fit **quant developer**
  roles first.

## 8. Key takeaways

1. A quant trader is the **live-market owner** of a strategy: execution, monitoring and risk.
2. A good strategy is **rules first**: designed, backtested, then run and watched.
3. **Python + probability + market sense + decisions under pressure** is the core skill set.
4. Researcher, trader and developer are different jobs that work closely together.
5. There is no single path in; **projects and proven experience** count as much as degrees.

## Applying it to my journal

- **Write every setup as rules** (entry, exit, size, invalidation) before trading it — if I
  can't write it as rules, I can't backtest it.
- **Daily routine log** that mirrors the quant day: data review → strategy adjustments →
  backtest notes → risk check.
- **Track risk metrics** every day: open exposure, daily P&L vs. limit, max drawdown.
- **Log overrides:** whenever I deviate from the rules (discretion), record why, then
  compare rule-based vs. overridden trades over time.
- **Log decisions under pressure:** note time-critical calls and how they turned out, to
  improve decision quality.
- **Skills tracker:** Python, statistics, options/futures knowledge — keep a learning log.
